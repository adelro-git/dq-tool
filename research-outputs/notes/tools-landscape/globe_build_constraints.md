## 2. Philippine regulatory considerations (DPA, NPC, NTC/SIM Registration, BSP, DICT)

### Takeaway
The Philippines has no general data localization mandate. Cross-border processing and outsourcing are allowed, but Globe as personal information controller (PIC) stays fully accountable. That means contracts or data sharing agreements with every processor (Vercel, AWS, Snowflake), security measures that meet NPC Circular 2023-06, 72-hour breach notification, DPO and data processing system registration, and a privacy impact assessment (PIA). SIM registration data has extra "absolute confidentiality" protection. For a DQ tool, the main design implication is to keep raw subscriber records and PII samples inside Globe's environment and show only aggregates or masked samples in the UI.

### Cited Findings
- **Cross-border transfers.** NPC Advisory 2024-01 provides model contractual clauses for cross-border transfers. They are recommended, not mandatory, and add no new obligations. There is no general prior-NPC-approval requirement for cross-border transfers, and the controller stays responsible for DPA compliance. — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/how-do-philippine-companies-comply-with-cross-border-data-transfers/); [InCorp guide](https://philippines.incorp.asia/guides/managing-third-party-data-privacy-risks/) (secondary; verify against the advisory on privacy.gov.ph)
- **Outsourcing.** Accountability rests with the controller, and NPC Circular 2023-06 (Security of Personal Data) is the current security baseline for controllers and processors. — [Chambers Technology & Outsourcing 2025](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/technology-and-outsourcing-2025/47/)
- NPC Circular 2023-06 sets minimum security requirements for both private and public sector entities. — [Baker McKenzie Global Data & Cyber Handbook](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/asia-pacific/philippines/topics/security-requirements-and-breach-notification)
- **Privacy Mark.** NPC Circular 2023-05 created a voluntary certification that covers secure cross-border transfers; it took effect 15 March 2024. — [Baker McKenzie InsightPlus](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/philippines-national-privacy-commission-issues-rules-for-philippine-privacy-mark-certification)
- **Breach notification.** The NPC and affected data subjects must be notified within 72 hours of knowledge or reasonable belief of a notifiable breach, under the DPA IRR and NPC Circular 16-03. A breach is notifiable when it involves sensitive personal information or information enabling identity fraud, there is reasonable belief of unauthorized acquisition, and there is a real risk of serious harm. Partial or initial notice with later supplements is allowed. — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/when-must-philippine-companies-report-data-breaches/); [Alburo Law](https://www.alburolaw.com/procedure-for-notifying-the-national-privacy-commission-in-case-of-data-privacy-breach/); [Baker McKenzie](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/asia-pacific/philippines/topics/security-requirements-and-breach-notification)
- **DPO and data processing system registration (NPC Circular 2022-04).** A covered PIC or PIP must register a newly implemented data processing system within 20 days on the NPC registration platform. Registration is renewed yearly, 30 days before expiry. Thresholds include 250+ employees or processing sensitive personal information of 1,000+ individuals; sources word the 1,000 trigger differently. — [NPC reminder](https://privacy.gov.ph/?p=43514); [Circular 2022-04 PDF](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Circular-2022-04.pdf); [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/a-ceos-guide-to-registration-of-data-processing-system-with-the-national-privacy-commission-part-1-legal-foundations-and-mandatory-requirements/)
- **SIM Registration Act (RA 11934).** Registration information is "absolutely confidential". Disclosure is allowed only by law, court order, subpoena tied to a sworn complaint, or the subscriber's written consent. Registration data is retained for 10 years after deactivation. Cyber-attacks on the SIM register must be reported to DICT within 24 hours. PTEs report annually to NTC, DICT and Congress. The IRR is NTC MC 001-12-2022 (12 Dec 2022). — [Grant Thornton PH](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/line-of-sight/smishing-no-more-an-overview-of-the-sim-card-registration-act/); [jur.ph summary](https://jur.ph/law/summary/subscriber-identity-module-sim-registration-act)
- Penalty figures conflict across sources (₱100k-₱1M progressive fines; up to ₱4M for confidentiality breaches; 6 months to 6 years imprisonment for some acts). — [Grant Thornton PH](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/line-of-sight/smishing-no-more-an-overview-of-the-sim-card-registration-act/); [digest.ph](https://www.digest.ph/laws/subscriber-identity-module-sim-registration-act)
- **BSP outsourcing (if GCash or fintech data is in scope).** BSP Circular 1137 (approved Feb 2022) tightened rules on outsourcing and IT risk. Arrangements are classified as material or non-material, inherent banking functions cannot be outsourced, and material outsourcing without approval is limited by supervisory rating. — [Philstar, Feb 2022](https://www.philstar.com/business/2022/02/21/2162141/rules-outsourcing-tightened); [Outsource Accelerator](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/bsp-amends-it-outsourcing-risk-management-rules/)
- A risk assessment is reportedly required before engaging a cloud provider. — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/navigating-cloud-outsourcing-legal-compliance-for-regulated-industries/)
- Google publishes a BSP IT Risk Management (MORB Sec. 148) compliance mapping for GCP. — [Google BSP ITRM mapping](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/bsp_itrm_gcp_compliancemapping.pdf)
- **Vercel's data location terms.** Vercel's DPA states it "may transfer data to and in the United States and anywhere else in the world where Vercel or its service providers maintain data processing operations". The default function region is US (iad1). — [Vercel Security & Compliance](https://vercel.com/docs/security/compliance)

### Inferences
- **Data minimization by design.** The DQ engine should compute metrics (null rates, row counts, distributions, freshness, schema drift) inside Globe's VPC and persist only aggregates. Failed-row samples, which typically contain MSISDN, IMSI, names, addresses or CDR details, should be off by default. Where enabled, samples should be masked or tokenized (hash MSISDN, truncate, k-anonymize), have short retention, and be gated by role.
- **SIM registration tables are the highest-risk dataset.** Profile them only with aggregate checks. Never ship sample rows to a third-party-hosted UI.
- **The DQ tool is itself a data processing system.** Expect to file or update an NPC registration entry and complete a PIA. If Vercel processes any personal data, a DPA or outsourcing agreement is needed under DPA Sec. 21/44 (accountability and outsourcing) [UNVERIFIED section numbers]. If the UI shows only aggregates and authenticated employee identity, the PIA scope shrinks, but employee identity data still counts as personal data.
- **Audit trails support breach response.** Log who viewed which sample, who changed which check, and who exported what. This supports the 72-hour assessment and the 24-hour DICT reporting for SIM-register incidents.
- **GCash/Mynt data adds BSP scope.** Including it likely makes Vercel or other hosting a BSP-relevant outsourcing or IT arrangement requiring materiality assessment. That is a strong reason to keep GCash out of the first version.

- **DICT and NTC.** I did not retrieve the DICT National Cybersecurity Plan 2023-2028 text or any NTC memorandum on telco cloud or data handling beyond the SIM IRR. [UNVERIFIED: the NCSP 2023-2028 classifies telecoms as critical information infrastructure (CII), which may bring incident reporting duties.]

## 3. Vercel suitability for an enterprise internal DQ tool

### Takeaway
Vercel is a good host for a Next.js UI and light API layer. It holds SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001:2022, has Singapore, Tokyo and Hong Kong regions, and the Enterprise plan adds SAML SSO, SCIM, RBAC and audit log drains. It is a poor fit for the check-execution engine. Functions are capped at 800s (1800s in beta, not available with Secure Compute). Cron is only a trigger, with a maximum of 100 per project. Private connectivity to Globe's data requires the Enterprise Secure Compute add-on: AWS VPC peering, or site-to-site VPN for GCP, Azure or on-prem. Vercel's DPA also allows worldwide data transfer. Use Vercel only for the UI, if at all, and keep all data-touching execution inside Globe's VPC.

### Cited Findings
- **Function limits (Fluid compute).** Hobby: 300s max. Pro and Enterprise: 300s default, 800s maximum, 1800s extended max in beta. Memory is 4 GB / 2 vCPU on Pro/Enterprise. Request/response payload is capped at 4.5 MB. Bundle size is 250 MB (500 MB for Python; 5 GB beta). There are 1,024 file descriptors shared across concurrent executions. The default region is iad1. Vercel suggests Vercel Workflows for unlimited-duration work. — [Vercel Functions Limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)
- Secure Compute and Static IPs do not support durations above 800s during the beta. — [Vercel Functions Limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)
- **Cron.** 100 cron jobs per project on all plans. Hobby is limited to once per day with ±59 min precision; Pro and Enterprise allow once per minute with per-minute precision. Cron simply invokes a Function, so function limits and pricing apply. — [Vercel Cron usage & pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing)
- **Regions.** 19 compute regions, including sin1 (Singapore, ap-southeast-1), hkg1 (Hong Kong), hnd1 (Tokyo), kix1 (Osaka), icn1 (Seoul), bom1 (Mumbai) and syd1 (Sydney). There is no Philippines region. There are 126 PoPs; a Manila PoP is not confirmed on the page. The platform primarily runs on AWS. — [Vercel regions](https://vercel.com/docs/regions); [Vercel Security & Compliance](https://vercel.com/docs/security/compliance)
- **Secure Compute availability.** It is a paid Enterprise add-on, now self-serve for some teams. It provides a dedicated VPC network with a static IP pair plus NAT gateway in a chosen region. AWS VPC peering is supported (non-overlapping CIDR, max 50 peerings per network). Site-to-site VPN covers Azure VNets, Google Cloud VPCs and on-prem data centres. — [Vercel Secure Compute](https://vercel.com/docs/networking/secure-compute)
- **Secure Compute operation.** Active/passive region failover is supported. Each project environment links to one active network in one region, with up to 100 projects per network. Vercel recommends credential auth on top of IP allowlisting. — [Vercel Secure Compute](https://vercel.com/docs/networking/secure-compute)
- **Secure Compute coverage.** It applies to Node.js, Ruby, Go and Python Functions and optionally builds. It does not apply to Routing Middleware or Edge Runtime functions. — [Vercel Secure Compute](https://vercel.com/docs/networking/secure-compute)
- **Secure Compute pricing.** Pricing is "contact sales". Private Data Transfer costs $0.15/GB for traffic leaving via the public internet; traffic over AWS VPC peering has no data transfer charge. — [Vercel Secure Compute](https://vercel.com/docs/networking/secure-compute)
- **Alternatives to Secure Compute.** Static IPs are a cheaper option for allowlisting only. AWS PrivateLink and BYOC (running Vercel Functions as Lambdas in your own AWS account) are also referenced as options. — [Vercel Secure Compute related links / BYOC guide](https://vercel.com/docs/networking/secure-compute) (BYOC not researched in depth)
- **Compliance.** Vercel holds SOC 2 Type 2 (Security, Confidentiality, Availability), ISO 27001:2022, PCI DSS AOCs (SAQ-D/SAQ-A), HIPAA BAA, the EU-US DPF and TISAX AL2. Data is encrypted with AES-256 at rest and TLS 1.3 in transit. Enterprise has isolated build infrastructure, and Vercel runs third-party pen tests. — [Vercel Security & Compliance](https://vercel.com/docs/security/compliance)
- **Data residency.** The DPA permits transfer to the US and anywhere Vercel or its subprocessors operate. Vercel's internal backups run every 2 hours with 30-day retention, replicated globally. — [Vercel Security & Compliance](https://vercel.com/docs/security/compliance)
- **Enterprise features.** SSO/SAML, Directory Sync (SCIM), Audit Logs plus Audit Log Drains, RBAC and Access Groups, deployment protection (Private Production Deployments, Trusted IPs, password protection), and a WAF with managed rulesets. Also: 3 days of runtime logs, configurable drains (Datadog, New Relic), SLAs, a dedicated success manager and multi-region failover for functions. — [Vercel Enterprise plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/enterprise)
- **Vercel Passport.** Vercel documents protecting internal apps behind your own IdP, such as Okta or Microsoft Entra ID. — [Vercel Enterprise plan related links](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/enterprise) (guide not fetched)
- **Resilience.** Vercel published an incident note for a service disruption on October 20, 2025, the date of a widely reported AWS us-east-1 outage. — [Vercel regions page related links](https://vercel.com/docs/regions) (details not fetched)

### Inferences
- **Execution must live elsewhere.** A DQ run over billions of CDR rows can take minutes to hours. Even pushed down into Snowflake SQL, orchestrating hundreds of checks, retries and backfills exceeds what Vercel Functions and Cron are designed for. Put the scheduler and workers (Airflow/MWAA, Dagster, ECS/Fargate tasks, Step Functions, or K8s CronJobs) inside Globe's AWS.
- **Reaching private warehouses from Vercel.** There are three ways, in order of preference. (1) Don't: the Vercel UI calls a Globe-hosted API (API Gateway or ALB behind a WAF, authenticated with OIDC/JWT from the corporate IdP), and only that API talks to Snowflake. (2) Secure Compute plus AWS VPC peering into a dedicated "DQ API" VPC, never directly into data VPCs. (3) Static IPs allowlisted on Snowflake network policies. Option 3 is the weakest because traffic crosses the public internet.
- **Keep PII out of Vercel.** Choosing sin1 and enabling Private Production Deployments reduces exposure, but the DPA's global-transfer clause and US-default posture mean Globe InfoSec will likely require that no subscriber PII ever transits Vercel functions. That constraint argues for aggregates-only APIs.
- **Cost.** Enterprise plus Secure Compute is a negotiated contract, typically well above Pro list prices (Enterprise pricing is not public). For a single internal tool with tens to hundreds of users, the incremental value over self-hosting Next.js on Globe's AWS is modest. Vercel's main benefits are developer experience and preview deployments.

### Gaps
- I found no published Enterprise or Secure Compute pricing. Vercel Pro per-seat pricing was not re-verified in this session.
- I did not confirm whether Secure Compute is offered in sin1 specifically. The docs say "a region you choose" among function regions, which suggests yes, but this is unverified.
- I did not retrieve Vercel's subprocessor list, the specific audit-log retention period, or SIEM-streaming formats.

## 4. Reference architecture options compared

### Takeaway
Option (c), fully inside Globe's AWS, is the default recommendation: a self-hosted Next.js UI on ECS Fargate/EKS (or App Runner) plus workers and a scheduler in the same VPC as Snowflake PrivateLink. It is the most compliant, simplest to clear with InfoSec and procurement, and has no execution limits. Option (b), UI on Vercel with workers in Globe's VPC, is viable if the team values Vercel's developer experience and the UI serves aggregates only, but it adds a vendor, an Enterprise contract and a second perimeter. Option (a), on-prem, only makes sense for sources that cannot leave the data centre (e.g., some network/OSS systems).

### Cited Findings
- Globe's stated direction is cloud-first on AWS, with a multi-year agreement renewed in November 2025 that includes platform engineering and disaster recovery. — [AWS press release](https://press.aboutamazon.com/aws/2025/11/globe-collaborates-with-aws-to-accelerate-digital-transformation); [BusinessWorld](https://bworldonline.com/?p=710637)
- Globe's EDW is Snowflake on AWS. — [Blazeclan](https://blazeclan.com/studies/blazeclans-data-ingestion-framework-helps-globe-telecom-inc-migrate-its-data-platform-to-snowflake-on-aws-within-3-months/)
- Vercel private connectivity requires Enterprise Secure Compute (VPC peering for AWS; VPN otherwise), and execution is capped at 800s with Secure Compute. — [Vercel Secure Compute](https://vercel.com/docs/networking/secure-compute); [Vercel Functions Limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)
- Globe's ISO 27001 scope includes Core Data Center Management, so Globe still runs on-prem data centres. — [Globe newsroom](https://www.globe.com.ph/about-us/newsroom/corporate/globe-iso-27001-2022-certification-digital-trust)

### Inferences — comparison matrix

| Dimension | (a) On-prem / private cloud containers | (b) UI on Vercel + workers in Globe VPC | (c) Fully in Globe cloud (ECS/EKS/App Runner; or Cloud Run/GKE if on GCP) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security / attack surface | Smallest external surface; relies on DC controls | Two perimeters; public Vercel endpoint (mitigate with SSO, Private Deployments, Trusted IPs, WAF); API must be exposed to Vercel | Single perimeter; can be internal-only via ALB plus corporate VPN or zero-trust |
| Network access to data | Good for on-prem sources; awkward for Snowflake on AWS (egress over Direct Connect) | Workers have native access; the UI reaches data only via API or Secure Compute peering | Native: Snowflake PrivateLink, S3 and IAM roles in the same account |
| Long-running / scheduled checks | Unlimited (K8s CronJobs, Airflow) | Unlimited in workers; Vercel cron only as a trigger (100/project, 800s) | Unlimited (MWAA/Airflow, Step Functions, EventBridge Scheduler plus Fargate tasks) |
| Cost | Capex/hardware plus ops; cheap marginal compute | Vercel Enterprise plus Secure Compute contract (not public), plus AWS worker cost | AWS pay-as-you-go; likely covered under Globe's AWS commit/EDP |
| Ops burden | Highest (patching, HA, hardware) | Medium (two platforms, two IAM models, two log pipelines) | Medium-low with managed services; platform engineering team likely exists |
| Compliance / procurement | Simplest for DPA; no new vendor | New third-party processor: vendor risk assessment, DPA, PIA update, possibly NPC DPS registration update | Reuses existing AWS/Snowflake DPAs and ISMS scope; no new vendor |
| Developer experience | Weakest | Best (preview deploys, instant rollbacks) | Good with CI/CD (GitHub Actions/CodePipeline); previews possible via ephemeral envs |
| Data residency | In-country | UI metadata may leave the region per Vercel DPA; PII must be kept out | Region-pinned (likely Singapore) under Globe's AWS controls |

- **Recommended pattern for (c).** Next.js `output: 'standalone'` container behind an internal ALB with OIDC authentication. A metadata DB (RDS Postgres) for check definitions, results and audit trail. Workers (Python, e.g., Great Expectations/Soda Core/dbt tests or custom SQL pushdown) on Fargate or EKS, scheduled by Airflow/MWAA or EventBridge. Secrets in AWS Secrets Manager with KMS. Snowflake access via PrivateLink and key-pair or OAuth service users with least-privilege roles (e.g., a `DQ_READER` role with masking policies applied). Alerts via SNS or Lambda to Slack/Teams/email and Jira/ServiceNow.
- **If (b) is chosen.** Use region sin1 and Secure Compute peered to a dedicated DQ API VPC. The API returns aggregates only. Vercel environment variables hold no warehouse credentials. Use SSO/SAML plus SCIM with Private Production Deployments. Forward audit log drains to Globe's SIEM.

## 5. Enterprise requirements (identity, secrets, audit, vuln/change management, vendor risk, integrations) and build vs. buy

### Takeaway
Expect these requirements:
- SSO through the corporate IdP (likely Entra ID or Google; unconfirmed), with RBAC mapped to IdP groups
- Cloud KMS/Secrets Manager or Vault for secrets
- Immutable audit logs forwarded to the SIEM
- SAST/DAST and container scanning
- ITIL-style change management (likely ServiceNow; unverified)
- A third-party vendor risk assessment for any new SaaS, which Vercel would trigger and AWS-native hosting avoids

Building is justified mainly by telco-specific checks and data-minimization control. A lean internal DQ platform typically needs about 3-6 FTE. This is a heuristic, not sourced from Globe.

- Vercel Enterprise provides SAML SSO, Directory Sync, RBAC and Access Groups, Audit Logs and Audit Log Drains, WAF and SLAs. Vercel Passport can front internal apps with Okta or Entra ID. — [Vercel Enterprise plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/enterprise)
- Vercel publishes compliance documents (SOC 2, ISO 27001, PCI AOC) via its Trust Center, security.vercel.com, which supports a vendor risk assessment questionnaire. — [Vercel Security & Compliance](https://vercel.com/docs/security/compliance)
- Globe's ISMS commits to Board reporting on data privacy, regulatory compliance and incident management, and aligns with ISO 27001, NIST and PCI-DSS. — [Globe ISMS policy](https://www.globe.com.ph/sites/default/files/2026-03/isms-pol-isp-001-01-globe-information-security-policy.pdf)

### Inferences
- **RBAC model.** Roles could be Viewer (aggregates only), Data Steward (manage checks for owned domains), PII-Sample Viewer (time-boxed, approval-based, logged) and Admin. Map them to IdP groups via SCIM. Enforce row and column masking in the warehouse (Snowflake masking/row-access policies) rather than only in the app.
- **Audit logging.** Log check changes (who, what, diff), runs, sample views and exports, and permission changes. Store logs append-only (e.g., S3 Object Lock or CloudTrail Lake) and forward them to the SIEM.
- **Encryption.** TLS 1.2+ everywhere, KMS-encrypted RDS/S3, and no PII in application logs.
- **Vulnerability and change management.** Dependency scanning (Dependabot/Snyk), container scanning (ECR/Inspector or Trivy), SAST, and annual pen test for an internet-exposed app. Releases go through CAB or standard-change templates. Infrastructure as Code (Terraform/CDK) gives an auditable change history.
- **Integrations.** Alert routing to Slack or MS Teams and email (Globe uses Google Workspace, so Google Chat/Gmail is also plausible). Incident creation in Jira or ServiceNow with dedup and alert-fatigue controls (severity, owners, SLAs per dataset).
- **Build vs. buy.** Buying (e.g., Monte Carlo, Soda Cloud, Acceldata, Collibra/Informatica DQ, Anomalo) speeds time-to-value but is itself a third-party processor that receives metadata and sometimes samples, so it faces the same vendor-risk/DPA path as Vercel. Building on open source (Great Expectations, Soda Core, dbt tests, Deequ) inside Globe's AWS keeps data in place. Rough sizing for a build: 1 product/tech lead, 2-3 data/backend engineers, 1 front-end, 0.5-1 platform/SRE, and part-time DPO/InfoSec liaison.
